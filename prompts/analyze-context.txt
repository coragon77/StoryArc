{{GENRE_BLOCK}}== YOUR TASK ==

Analyze this story deeply. Do not just tell me what happened -- tell me what is HAPPENING. Identify what is simmering beneath the surface. Find the threads that have been set up but not paid off. Understand what the story is ABOUT at its core -- not the plot events, but the thematic currents running beneath them.

Think systematically through each of these analytical lenses:

1. NARRATIVE MOMENTUM -- What direction is the story naturally moving? What feels inevitable? What feels like it could go either way? Where is the narrative energy concentrated? Is the story building toward something specific, or drifting in search of direction? If it's drifting, what anchors could give it focus?

2. UNFINISHED BUSINESS -- What promises has the story made to the reader that it hasn't fulfilled yet? Every time a story introduces a mystery, raises a question, creates a tension, or establishes a "Chekhov's gun," it makes an implicit promise. Track these promises. What questions have been raised but not answered? What tensions have been created but not resolved? What characters have been introduced but not fully explored? Be specific -- name characters, events, and situations.

3. CHARACTER TRAJECTORIES -- Where is each major character heading? What do they want, both consciously and unconsciously? What are they afraid of? What internal conflicts are they wrestling with? How have their relationships shifted recently? What relationships are stable vs. under pressure? Which characters are growing, which are stagnating, and which are on the edge of a transformation? Pay attention to the PLAYER CHARACTER specifically -- they are the lens through which the story is experienced, and arcs should challenge them.

4. POWER DYNAMICS -- Who holds power? Who is gaining it? Who is losing it? What alliances are forming or fracturing? What institutions, factions, or forces are exerting pressure on the characters? Power dynamics create natural story engines: when power shifts, stories happen. Look for power vacuums, contested authority, rising underdogs, and crumbling hierarchies.

5. EMOTIONAL UNDERCURRENTS -- What emotions are running through the story right now? Is there grief that hasn't been processed? Trust that hasn't been tested? Desire that hasn't been acknowledged? Guilt, fear, ambition, loyalty, resentment -- what is driving characters beneath their surface actions? The strongest arcs tap into these emotional currents rather than fighting against them.

6. THEMATIC WEIGHT -- What is this story actually exploring? Not "fantasy adventure" but the real themes: autonomy vs. duty, the cost of power, the nature of identity, what it means to belong, the tension between safety and freedom. What themes have been present from the start? What new themes are emerging? What themes have been explored well and could be set aside, vs. what themes are just beginning to develop?

7. WORLD STATE -- Beyond the immediate character drama, what is happening in the broader world? Political shifts, seasonal changes, economic pressures, approaching threats, distant events that could ripple inward. The world should feel alive and in motion, not just a backdrop for character drama. What external forces could disrupt the current status quo?

8. TONAL TRAJECTORY -- What does the story feel like right now? Is it getting darker, lighter, more complex, more intimate, more epic? What tonal shifts are in progress? What tone has the story been sustaining for a while that might need a change to avoid fatigue? A story that has been dark for a long time might need a moment of warmth -- not to abandon its tone, but to give contrast that makes the darkness hit harder.

9. WHAT THE STORY NEEDS -- Based on everything above, what does the story need NEXT to stay compelling? More external pressure? A quieter character moment? A revelation? A betrayal? An arrival? A departure? A complication that forces characters to choose between two things they value? What would a skilled novelist do here -- not just to advance the plot, but to deepen the reader's investment in these characters and this world?

10. GAPS AND BLIND SPOTS -- What aspects of the world or characters have been established but never explored? Is there a faction that's been mentioned but never encountered? A character relationship that's been stated but never tested? A rule of the world that's been established but never challenged? These gaps are opportunities -- they represent unexplored territory that feels grounded because it's already been set up.

== QUALITY GUIDELINES ==

SPECIFICITY IS EVERYTHING. Compare these two analyses:

BAD: "There is tension between the main characters."
GOOD: "Elara and Marcus are operating under a fragile alliance born from necessity -- she doesn't trust his connections to House Valdris, and he hasn't forgiven her for the Thornfield incident. They function well in crisis but avoid being alone together. The next time they're forced into close quarters without an external threat to unite them, the cracks will show."

The bad version could describe any story. The good version could only describe THIS story.

ADDITIONAL GUIDELINES:
- Identify at least 3-5 active threads. Long-running stories always have multiple threads in play simultaneously.
- Don't just look at what's obvious. The most interesting narrative opportunities often come from threads that have been quietly building in the background -- the secondary character whose loyalty has never been tested, the faction that's been mentioned but never confronted, the promise that was made and then forgotten.
- If the context is sparse (short chat, few lorebook entries), work with what you have -- but be honest about gaps in your understanding. Extrapolate where reasonable, but flag uncertainty.
- Your analysis should make a novelist think "yes, THIS is exactly where my story is and these are exactly the threads I should be pulling" -- not "this is a generic summary anyone could write about any story."
- Characters in collaborative RP are not plot devices. They are the point. Your analysis should treat character dynamics as the primary engine of narrative, with plot events as the fuel.
- Look for IRONY -- situations where characters' actions are producing the opposite of what they intend. These are goldmines for compelling narrative.
- Look for CONVERGENCE -- situations where multiple unrelated threads are about to collide. These are your best opportunities for dramatic arc starts.
