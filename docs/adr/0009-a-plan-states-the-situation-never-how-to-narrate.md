# A plan states the situation, never how to narrate

A plan is written by a model and sits in the narrator's prompt every turn for the length of the arc, so its wording becomes the story's. In the Paragon chat (2026-11-08) three replies broke the world in the same way: an ally tied her help to a keycard and a blood match (message 3023), a Ward team lead produced a registry form and ordered an adult Defender around (3051), and a PR coordinator held a signed agreement in front of a volunteer to pull her out of school (3069). What the narrator got from the active acts was their genre ("… non-con, corruption, sexual deviancy, … political thriller"), a premise promising "legal traps" and "institutional exploitation", long pressures that staged scenes, and a military rank in a pressure of a story where the agency is not the military. A test on the disposable test chat (2026-11-09) isolated the plan's wording: with pressures written as contract clauses the narrator invented a contract in 6 of 6 replies, with pressures written as lever and cost in 0 of 3, the same genre line in the prompt both times.

So a plan says what is true in the world: what is possible, who holds what power, what a character or faction wants, believes or knows. It never says how to narrate: tone, pacing, emphasis, how a character comes across, what to show or leave out. Character portrayal stays with the player's material (the character card, the lorebook, the preset); a change the arc itself causes is situation and may stay. Genre and tone steer generation only and never reach the narrator. Someone who presses does it with leverage the setting gives them, never with authority they do not hold, and the planner and the plan check name the lever and the cost, never the move. Story Arc's own code-written text (section headers, pacing and phase lines) is outside this decision: the risk is planner text nobody reviewed, not instructions as such.

The fix is story-agnostic: nothing assumes where a player keeps their rules, because a player's rules may be in the preset, the Author's Note, the lorebook or nowhere.

## Considered options

- Feed the planner the player's Author's Note as standing rules: rejected by the player as a fix for one story; it assumes where a player keeps rules.
- A sentence ranking the plan below the narration instructions and the established story: tested, no effect (3 of 3 failures with and without it); not shipped.
- A setting that sends tone to the narrator for players whose preset says little about style: rejected; style belongs to the preset.
- Keep the genre line in the injection as a guard (ADR-0004): dropped; it neither caused nor prevented the failure in the test, and the player ruled that style never reaches the story.

## Consequences

Directives and anti-patterns give way to a `worldFacts` list in new plans, and an arc's tone, directives and anti-patterns are no longer printed for the narrator; re-planning an arc converts it (design: `docs/plans/2026-11-09-plans-state-the-situation-design.md`, Part B). A player whose preset says little about style loses the tone and directives an episode carried; Stefan accepted that trade-off (2026-11-09). The plan check's job grows from give-aways to authority the setting does not grant; its exception for real authority asks whether a person in that place holds the power in a setting like this one (an officer commands soldiers; an employer does not own an employee's free time), and the premise the planner wrote never grants a power by itself.

## Amendment 2026-11-09: the plan agrees with the story and the author's register

A plan from the Paragon chat (2026-11-09) passed the check with three faults. A world fact called the main character "an uncertified minor Ward" while the knowledge tracker held her age as 18, set by the player: the planner and the check saw the cast's names only, never what the story had established about them. A world fact said she "draws intense, predatory fixation from peers, superiors, and VIPs alike": it reads as a fact, so it passed, and the narrator makes a claim about everyone true of every character in every scene. A pressure said "submitting turns her into a high-society plaything" where the player wanted pressure through deniable hints nobody admits. So the check's step 2 gains three rules. A pressure, trigger action, world fact or unrevealed secret that contradicts a cast member's detail (age, role, relation) or another world fact is rewritten to agree; a secret may be rewritten for this as well as for leverage, keeping what it is. A world fact names who holds which power or who wants or believes what, never everyone or every group of people; one that does is rewritten to name who, or dropped. When the direction or the author's answers to the wizard's questions say how pressure works or how hard the arc is, an item in another register is rewritten into the one the author asked for, keeping what it is about. The cast the planner and the check see now carries each member's current details from the knowledge ledger, a relation by the other character's name; while character details are off it carries none, because the ledger stops keeping them up to date. The author's answers reach the check under the premise and direction as public author input. The planner gets the same two expectations up front: a world fact makes no claim about everyone and agrees with the cast, and every pressure leaves a choice that costs, which no ally can dissolve in one conversation. The rules name no story's content and need no new call.

## Amendment 2026-11-09 (evening): acts and sagas print every world fact

The cut to the first three world facts in an act or saga section is gone. It was set while an act printed every open milestone pressure; since ADR-0010 an act prints one, so the section stays short with every fact. The cut also decided which facts the narrator lost: in the Paragon chat a re-plan ranked a new fact second and pushed "Ellie is an adult volunteer: nobody at SWORD has a say over whom she meets … the people who want something from her find it easy to look away" to fourth, out of the prompt, twice. Stefan: "why are you only inserting the first three facts?" and "yes, remove the limit". The planner's ranking sentence and the re-plan's "in this order" instruction went with it.
