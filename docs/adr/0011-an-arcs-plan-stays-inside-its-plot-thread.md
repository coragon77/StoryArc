# An arc's plan stays inside its plot thread; hidden links are the player's choice

ADR-0010 made an arc one plot thread, but the planner was told so only for milestones. For an episode nothing said it, and the outline prompt asked for the opposite: its principle "Complementary, not redundant" told the planner to "create something that intersects", and its framing of the running arcs let a new arc "intersect with them". In a live chat the player planned a short personal episode for their character with a one-line direction, while a friend's rise to fame ran in the same story without an arc of its own. The planner gave the episode two of seven scene beats whose fulfillment was an event of the friend's story, a climax beat that waited on both, a premise that asked the friend's question beside the episode's own, and a secret and a trigger about the friend's antagonist. The wizard had asked how the episode should "balance with" the friend's events; the player answered that they should be the stage for it, and the planner read the stage as content. The player played the episode to its last scene, and the tracker stopped after three of seven beats, waiting for the other thread's events in the form the planner had pictured. For about 165 messages the narrator got the other thread's pressure under the episode's name. The owner's rulings, 2026-11-26: the arcs are separate and their interplay belongs to the chat; a beat that waits on another thread is never allowed; a hidden link the planner makes is welcome, and a checkbox lets the player forbid it.

So an arc's plan stays inside its own plot thread, at every tier. The thread is what the direction, the author's answers and, on a re-plan, the rulings name; with a blank direction on a new arc the planner picks one thread and states it in the premise, and on a re-plan the premise the wizard passes in defines it. Everything else going on in the story is **backdrop** for this arc: it may supply where and when a beat plays, never what happens in it. An event the direction or an answer names is backdrop unless the text says its outcome is part of this arc. A thread needs no arc of its own to count as another thread. Always, at every tier: a fulfillment is a step of this arc's thread, never another thread's event, and no beat waits on one; a pressure names a force of this thread; the premise asks this thread's question only; a secret, trigger or clue is about this arc's own people and things. The test given to the model: take the other thread out of the story, and every beat must still be fulfillable and every secret must still say something about this arc's own people.

A **link** is a secret that ties this arc's own people or things to another thread ("the robbers secretly work for the big bad"). It is the one place where threads may meet in a plan, and it is the player's choice: the wizard's Direction step has a checkbox, **Keep this arc to itself**, on for every new arc. On, the planner invents no link. Off, it may, and the player is not told whether it did. Either way a secret about another thread's people with nothing of this arc in it is not a link and is never planned. A link lives only in a secret and its clues and triggers, never in a beat. The plan check is the second line (ADR-0006, amendment 2026-11-26): it removes what the planner let through.

## Considered options

- A wizard question about how this arc relates to the others, instead of the checkbox: rejected. The wizard asked exactly that in the case above, in free text, and the answer was misread. The questions prompt now never asks whether or how another thread's events belong to the arc; where or when it plays may still be asked.
- A warning to the player instead of removal: rejected. The player would have to read the plan to act on it, which is what the plan check exists to avoid ("The plan is secret from me", GOALS.md).
- Separation as a per-arc choice: rejected. A beat that waits on another thread is never wanted; only a link is a choice, so the checkbox governs links and nothing else.
- Measuring separation against the other arcs instead of the direction: rejected. When the episode above was planned, the other thread had no arc, so a rule to stay out of the other arcs would have had nothing to stay out of.

## Consequences

The rule is written once, in a block built in code from the setting, and filled into the outline prompt and the plan check prompt, so the two cannot drift apart; the outline's "intersect" wording and its "multi-thread" length guidance go, and the tier block's episode branch says "one plot thread" as the milestone branch does. The block says "plot thread" throughout, because the prompts also use "threads" for loose ends. The setting is stored on the arc only when links are allowed (`allowLinks: true`); an arc without it is kept to itself, so arcs planned before this decision need no migration and a re-plan of one takes the other thread's material out of the road. The situation stays as ADR-0003 has it: completed beats, revealed secrets, planted clues and fired triggers are kept whatever thread they belonged to. A new arc always starts with the checkbox on and is not prefilled from the newest arc, unlike genre and themes; a re-plan shows the stored value. There is no Arc Manager control: the setting acts only when a plan is generated. An edited outline or plan check prompt is told that its built-in version changed and that the new placeholder is missing. World facts and the character list are untouched: the threads share the world and the cast. Threads still meet in the chat: when one arc's events land in the story, another arc that recognises its own milestone in the same messages moves. What the narrator is sent does not change. A re-plan of an episode writes only the scenes that remain, at least one, as acts and sagas already do, so a late re-plan no longer pads the road with invented obstacles. The owner ruled on the rule itself, the checkbox and its default, and removal by the plan check; spec #97 decided, open to the owner's veto, that the check may rewrite a planner-written premise and gets the analysis on a new plan, the guards on removal, that the questions stop asking about overlap, that the checkbox is never prefilled, the step log's count, and that there is no Arc Manager control. Not solved here: scene beats played out of order still stall an episode, and an episode's message clock still counts messages spent on other threads.
