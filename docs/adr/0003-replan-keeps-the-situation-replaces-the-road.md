# Re-plan keeps the situation and replaces the road

An arc has two layers with different lifetimes. The situation (premise, secrets, fulfilled beats, planted foreshadowing, fired triggers) stays true whatever the player does; the road (unfulfilled beats, unplanted foreshadowing, unfired triggers, and the guidance written for them: directives, anti-patterns, tone, themes, escalation, estimated length, tier) is one predicted path and goes stale when the story diverges. Re-plan therefore never regenerates secrets (the model may only add), keeps every fulfilled item with its id, and replaces the rest from a fresh three-phase generation that sees the current chat. Title and premise are player-edited, never model-regenerated. We rejected letting the model judge secrets as "contradicted": it requires the blind player to review a diff, and a contradicted secret is harmless when simply never revealed.

## Amendment 2026-10-25: re-plan may grow the tier, never shrink it

Re-plan offers the arc's own tier or a larger one: an episode can become an act or saga, an act a saga, a saga stays a saga. Shrinking was possible before and is now refused in the wizard (`replanTiers` in `replan.js`). The kept situation is the reason. A saga's fulfilled milestones are states the story reached in any order; an episode's beats are ordered scenes with one active at a time and a pacing clock, so kept milestones have no meaning inside an episode. Growing works the other way round: completed scene beats stay completed and print nothing in the milestone section, and the new milestones are all active. A story that needs a scene-level episode inside a saga gets a new episode arc next to the saga. It also keeps background focus (ADR-0001 amendment) on acts and sagas only, without a rule for what a background arc becomes as an episode.

## Amendment 2026-11-04: the model proposes the premise, the player decides

"Title and premise are player-edited, never model-regenerated" becomes: title and premise are the player's. On a re-plan the model proposes a premise from the story as it is now. The player sees it next to the current one and accepts, edits or rejects it before anything is stored. Without rulings the current premise is pre-selected. Phase 2 has always asked for a premise, and `mergeReplan` discarded it; that generated premise is now the proposal, carried on the merged arc as the transient `proposedPremise` and removed by `applyReplan`, which stores the chosen text and refuses a blank one. No call is added. A premise that carried a development the player had closed survived every re-plan unless the player rewrote it by hand (live re-plans in the Paragon chat, 2026-11-04). The title stays the player's alone: it is the arc's name in every list.

## Amendment 2026-11-04: rulings belong to the situation

On a re-plan the model proposes the premise from the stored rulings and the story as it is now; with rulings the proposal is pre-selected. Rulings, the out-of-character lines in which the player overruled the plan, belong to the situation: a re-plan never drops one by itself and passes all of them to the planner. Only the player removes one. All three calls get them as the player's binding word: Phase 1 under the re-planned arc as "Overruled by the player:", Phase 2 in the arc-so-far block with the instruction to propose a premise that follows every ruling, and the plan check as public author input next to premise and direction. Applying the re-plan marks the open rulings answered; answered rulings stay on the arc and reach every later re-plan, so one re-plan is not undone by the next. A ruling the player removes in the re-plan dialog reaches no call and leaves the arc only when the re-plan is applied. Rulings had not reached the planner before only because the harvest window (the last 50 messages) had moved past them and the kept premise restated what they overruled; the player typed them into the direction field by hand (Paragon chat, 2026-11-04).
