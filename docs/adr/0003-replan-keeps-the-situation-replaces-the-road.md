# Re-plan keeps the situation and replaces the road

An arc has two layers with different lifetimes. The situation (premise, secrets, fulfilled beats, planted foreshadowing, fired triggers) stays true whatever the player does; the road (unfulfilled beats, unplanted foreshadowing, unfired triggers, and the guidance written for them: directives, anti-patterns, tone, themes, escalation, estimated length, tier) is one predicted path and goes stale when the story diverges. Re-plan therefore never regenerates secrets (the model may only add), keeps every fulfilled item with its id, and replaces the rest from a fresh three-phase generation that sees the current chat. Title and premise are player-edited, never model-regenerated. We rejected letting the model judge secrets as "contradicted": it requires the blind player to review a diff, and a contradicted secret is harmless when simply never revealed.

## Amendment 2026-10-25: re-plan may grow the tier, never shrink it

Re-plan offers the arc's own tier or a larger one: an episode can become an act or saga, an act a saga, a saga stays a saga. Shrinking was possible before and is now refused in the wizard (`replanTiers` in `replan.js`). The kept situation is the reason. A saga's fulfilled milestones are states the story reached in any order; an episode's beats are ordered scenes with one active at a time and a pacing clock, so kept milestones have no meaning inside an episode. Growing works the other way round: completed scene beats stay completed and print nothing in the milestone section, and the new milestones are all active. A story that needs a scene-level episode inside a saga gets a new episode arc next to the saga. It also keeps background focus (ADR-0001 amendment) on acts and sagas only, without a rule for what a background arc becomes as an episode.

## Amendment 2026-11-04: the model proposes the premise, the player decides

"Title and premise are player-edited, never model-regenerated" becomes: title and premise are the player's. On a re-plan the model proposes a premise from the story as it is now. The player sees it next to the current one and accepts, edits or rejects it before anything is stored. Without rulings the current premise is pre-selected. Phase 2 has always asked for a premise, and `mergeReplan` discarded it; that generated premise is now the proposal, carried on the merged arc as the transient `proposedPremise` and removed by `applyReplan`, which stores the chosen text and refuses a blank one. No call is added. A premise that carried a development the player had closed survived every re-plan unless the player rewrote it by hand (live re-plans in the Paragon chat, 2026-11-04). The title stays the player's alone: it is the arc's name in every list.

## Amendment 2026-11-04: rulings belong to the situation

On a re-plan the model proposes the premise from the stored rulings and the story as it is now; with rulings the proposal is pre-selected. Rulings, the out-of-character lines in which the player overruled the plan, belong to the situation: a re-plan never drops one by itself and passes all of them to the planner. Only the player removes one. All three calls get them as the player's binding word: Phase 1 under the re-planned arc as "Overruled by the player:", Phase 2 in the arc-so-far block with the instruction to propose a premise that follows every ruling, and the plan check as public author input next to premise and direction. Applying the re-plan marks the open rulings answered; answered rulings stay on the arc and reach every later re-plan, so one re-plan is not undone by the next. A ruling the player removes in the re-plan dialog reaches no call and leaves the arc only when the re-plan is applied. Rulings had not reached the planner before only because the harvest window (the last 50 messages) had moved past them and the kept premise restated what they overruled; the player typed them into the direction field by hand (Paragon chat, 2026-11-04).

## Amendment 2026-11-09: a re-plan may propose new wording for unrevealed secrets

A re-plan may also propose new wording for secrets not yet revealed; the player takes or keeps each, and its id, holders and reveal phase stay. Secrets that came out are never rewritten. The proposal comes from the plan check, which rewrites a secret that has someone force a character through a power they would not hold in the setting, keeping what the secret is and every planted clue it explains; no call is added. For a secret the arc already had, the rewrite is carried on the merged arc as the transient `proposedContent`, shown in step 3 next to the stored text and the planted clues that point at the secret, and removed by `applyReplan` whatever the choice; keep is the default. A secret the re-plan itself adds, like every secret of a new plan, is rewritten directly: no player has seen it yet. A secret's wording is in the narrator's prompt for the whole arc, and before this amendment secrets were add-only, so a wrong one stayed until it came out (ruling, 2026-11-09).

## Amendment 2026-11-09 (later): a re-plan plans the unrevealed secrets again

This supersedes the earlier amendment of the same day: there are no secret proposals and no keep-or-take choice. A secret not yet revealed is road, not situation. The player's ruling: "a secret that has not entered the story yet can be rewritten. Because if I replan I am telling the extension to recreate the steps of the arc that did not happen. Which also includes secrets. So its really fine for me? Any reason the keep it that complicated?" Revealed (disclosed) secrets stay exactly as they are. Every other secret is planned again with the rest of the road: the arc-so-far block lists each one with its id, its holders, the planted clues pointing at it and the fired triggers linked to it, and the planner keeps, rewrites or drops it. A secret with planted clues or fired triggers must be kept or rewritten so it still explains them: the player has seen those clues. `mergeReplan` keeps the revealed secrets and takes every other secret from the generated plan; one under an old id replaces the stored secret, so its clues and triggers stay attached by id. A dropped secret is gone, and a kept clue or fired trigger that pointed at it points at no known secret, which nothing reads as a link. The plan check applies its secret rewrites directly, on a re-plan too, and its register rule covers unrevealed secrets. The proposal machinery (`proposedContent`, the review step's secret choice, `secretChoices` in `applyReplan`) is removed. The original reason for add-only secrets (a blind player would have to review a diff) no longer holds: nobody reviews anything, and a secret that never entered the story has nothing to contradict. Two guards keep a rewrite off what came out: the plan check's revealed list judges the arc's secrets in their wording before the re-plan, and a secret it finds revealed comes back as stored, disclosed, whatever the planner wrote under its id (a secret can come out on screen without ever being marked disclosed); and an id validation invented because the planner gave none never replaces a stored secret, since older arcs carry those positional ids.

## Amendment 2026-11-26: a grown episode's scene beats leave the beat list

This overrules one sentence of the 2026-10-25 amendment: "completed scene beats stay completed and print nothing in the milestone section". That covered what the narrator sees. Everything else that counts or shows beats read the kept scene beats as milestones. The phase started an act about two thirds into its curve, and the stall nudge fired about three times too early. The beat strip said "Milestone 1 of 9" with three milestones. The JSON editor refused an old scene beat without a pressure, the status toggle could reopen one as the current milestone, and a later re-plan counted them toward the milestone count (#90). A mark on the kept beats (`sceneBeat`) was tried and reverted: it fixed only the count, and every other place would have needed the same exception.

Now `mergeReplan` moves an episode's closed scene beats into `episodeBeats` when the re-plan grows it into an act or saga. The act starts with only its own milestones, like a fresh act. Only the planning side reads `episodeBeats`: both ARC SO FAR blocks print them as already happened (the generation call in a block of their own, outside the milestone count), and the knowledge extraction gets them among the done beats, as before. The JSON editor keeps the field as stored and does not show it. An arc that grew before this change cannot be found: nothing records an arc's earlier tier, and a scan of every chat on 2026-11-12 found none.

## Amendment 2026-11-26 (later): a planted clue does not keep a secret of another plot thread

A live re-plan of an old mixed arc kept two secrets of other plot threads (ADR-0011), because each had a clue already planted: the planner was told to keep them, and the plan check could not remove them either (ADR-0006). Now the plan check may remove such a secret as foreign, behind its guards on secrets that came out (ADR-0006, amendment of the same date). The planner's rule of the 2026-11-09 (later) amendment stays as it was: "A secret with planted clues or fired triggers must be kept or rewritten so it still explains them." The decision that such a secret belongs to another thread is the check's alone, so that it is always taken behind those guards and logged. A first version let the planner drop it too; the review reverted that, since nothing guarded the planner's drop. As a backstop, the merge brings back an unrevealed secret the plan left out when a planted clue or a fired trigger points at it, so it always reaches the check. When the check removes it, the planted clue and the fired trigger stay stored, as the situation is: they happened. They then point at no known secret, which the earlier amendment already allows, and their secret's id stays taken: a later re-plan suffixes a new secret that would get it, so no new secret inherits what was planted for another. This was decided under the owner's delegation on spec #97, and stays open to the owner's veto.
