# Plans are checked for give-aways before play

A planned trigger told the narrator to have a character "panic over her brother's clinic scholarship, creating an opening for Ellie to flip her", while the same section listed that fact under SECRETS as "do not reveal". The narrator followed the specific instruction and the character confessed (Paragon chat, 2026-10-30). Two fixes that only added instructions failed: a header sentence on the TRIGGERS block, and generator rules against secrets and payoffs in triggers and pressures. The re-planned act still carried a trigger that stated a climax secret, a payoff clause and a pressure that stated an escalation secret. Cleaning that up by hand means the player reads the secrets, which the extension exists to avoid ("The plan is secret from me", GOALS.md).

So every generated plan goes through a third, narrow LLM call before it is stored. The call sees the secrets, the unfired triggers, the open pressures, the premise and the author's direction, and on a re-plan the Phase 1 analysis and the last ten story messages. It rewrites trigger actions and pressures that give a hidden secret away or name a payoff, decides which of the arc's secrets the story has revealed (re-plan only, both ways, so a reveal the user deleted or regenerated is taken back), and ties triggers to the secret they touch. A trigger tied to a secret is shown only once that secret is. The premise and the direction are passed as public, because a first live run rewrote items that carried out the author's own direction. A failed or unreadable check keeps the plan as generated.

## Considered options

- A word-overlap check in code: tried on the Paragon plans and rejected. A harmless pressure shared 4 words with its secret, the two leaks 5 each, and 150 of the chat's item–secret pairs shared 4 or more.
- More rules in the generator prompt only: they slipped on 3 of 8 new items in the first re-plan that had them.
- Manual cleanup in the Arc Manager: spoils the plan for the player.
- A per-secret "may come out only when …" condition printed next to the secret: a stated condition is a destination, and the narrator can contrive it ("Starve the solver").

## Consequences

Every plan costs one more call on the generation profile. The check judges meaning, so it can still miss a give-away or rewrite something harmless; one live run before and one after the direction fix are the evidence so far. Milestone and beat intentions are not checked: they are printed in the beat check, and rewriting one would change what "fulfilled" means.

## Amendment 2026-11-04: the check also rewrites fulfillments that can never be recognised

Beats are recognised from a message or two at a time (ADR-0008), so a fulfillment that is a tally of many scenes or a chain of separate events joined by "and" never shows in one message, and its beat stays open for good. The generator now asks for one on-screen moment per fulfillment, and the check is the second line, as it is for give-aways: it receives the plan's open fulfillments and rewrites a tally or a chain into the one moment at which the development shows, keeping the beat id and its meaning. The reason given above for leaving intentions alone ("rewriting one would change what 'fulfilled' means") holds only for beats already in play. The check runs before the plan is stored, so nothing has been recognised against the new text yet. On a re-plan the check runs after the merge, and completed beats the merge kept are not passed to it. Still one call.

## Amendment 2026-11-04: the check also renames a new character who got a taken name

The planner invents characters, and before the cast reached it, it could not see who was already in the story. In the two re-plans of the Street act it changed Priya Mehta to Priya Patel and produced a "Toby Vance-Miller", and the stored plan named a street punk "Marco Vega", sharing Marco with one record and Vega with another. A colliding name in a plan is worse than one in a reply: the narrator reads the plan on every message for a whole act, and the plan is secret, so the player does not see it to fix it. The planner now gets the cast (issue #44), and the check is the second line, as it is for give-aways (issue #49).

While the knowledge tracker is on, the check also receives the cast with aliases, the taken names and the plan's own list of characters, and answers renames, pairs of from and to: a new character with a taken first name or surname whom the plan does not make related to the character who has it, or a cast member named differently from the cast, gets one name throughout the plan. Unlike the other rewrites, a rename reaches every text field of the plan, secrets included, because a character must carry one name everywhere. Completed beats, fired triggers, the direction and the genre stay as they are. The check still judges, and code guards what it may rename: never a cast name or alias, never a name in the direction or in the premise the wizard passed in on a re-plan, never to a name that uses a taken name, never a fuller form of a single-word cast record into a new person, and never a name the plan does not use. A rename replaces whole names only. Still one call. With the tracker off the cast is empty, and the check is as before.

A note on ADR-0003: a re-plan never regenerates a secret, but a rename can change the wording of a kept secret, when that secret names a new character from the earlier plan who has a taken name and has not entered the ledger. The secret's content is otherwise unchanged. This is recorded here rather than as an ADR-0003 amendment, because the merge itself does not change.

## Amendment 2026-11-12: a process milestone is fulfilled once it has taken hold

A milestone that is a process the story lives through over many scenes (a honeymoon, a slow slide, trust growing) was written with a fulfillment its first occurrence already met: the Paragon act's honeymoon, "Ellie has experienced the tangible rewards…", closed 25 messages after a re-plan, and its saga re-plan repeated it even though the direction said "the good stretch comes first and lasts". The one-moment rule above stays, since a tally can never be recognised. What changes is which moment a milestone names: a process milestone names the moment that shows the process has taken hold, a one-off event (a death, a betrayal, a first meeting) the event itself. The TIER block says so for acts and sagas, and the check's step 3 rewrites a process milestone's fulfillment that the first scene of its pressure would meet; the code passes that rule to the check (`{{PROCESS_MILESTONE_RULE}}`) only for milestones, ahead of its "leave alone" clause. Episode scene beats are meant to be met in one scene and are left alone. Blind count on two Paragon directions, two plans each from the same analysis: first-occurrence process milestones fell from 6 to 2–3 in four plans, and both re-generated honeymoons became "has come to rely on her privileges".
